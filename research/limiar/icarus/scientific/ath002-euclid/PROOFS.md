## T01/T02: prefix selection
Let P_i={1,...,i}, q_i=floor(i(b-4)/28), q_0=0, b in {8,16,24,32}. Cap K optional, Q_i=min(q_i,K). Feasible sets satisfy |S intersect P_i|<=Q_i. Objective max cardinality then max sum of finite priorities (negative absolute margin in ICARO). Existing heap proof applies to arbitrary real priorities with deterministic index tie order. Logical cost C_i=4i+28|S intersect P_i|<=bi. n=64,b=16 gives 27 expansions, 1012 terms, 12 unused. No physical CPU quota.
Exchange argument: for feasible X,Y with |X|<|Y|, assume every y in Y minus X cannot be added to X. Each such y belongs to a tight prefix of X. Largest such prefix contains all Y minus X. Feasibility implies Y has at most as many elements as X in that prefix; outside it Y is a subset of X. Contradiction. Heredity and exchange prove a matroid on finite indices; restricted ground sets and truncations preserve feasibility structure. This is established mathematics, not novelty.
## T03: forced audit contraction
Given feasible mandatory A, optional S disjoint from A, define r_i=Q_i-|A intersect P_i|, R_i=min_{j>=i} r_j. For optional prefix counts f_i nondecreasing, f_i<=r_i for all i iff f_i<=R_i for all i: necessity follows f_i<=f_j<=r_j; sufficiency R_i<=r_i. Thus suffix tightening preserves exactly the feasible optional sets. Scan available indices only, insert and remove worst until heap size<=R_i. R nondecreasing, hence prior constraints remain valid. For each prefix, induction on eligible inserts shows every k<=heap size smallest-weight (or largest-priority) heap subset is optimal feasible size k; if new index included use best k-1 previously feasible, otherwise best k. Trim leaves all retained k optima unchanged. For cardinality, exchange above implies any feasible A extends to a base of size Q_n (full ground set); if further masks restrict the ground set, maximum may be smaller. In ARGOS/THEMIS A is always feasible: earliest four zero-based positions 2,16,32,48 satisfy quota at each insertion (1,7,14,21); earliest two 2,32 satisfy (1,14); later choices only relax. The actual ARGOS extend uses uncapped HORIZON.
## T04: HT estimator
Condition on the batch (including fixed outcomes for evaluation), past delivered history, model, chosen number of strata and plain candidate sets. Define h_i=(1[i in C]-1[i in M])*g_i, g_i=1[full correct]-1[lite correct]. Delta=100/n sum h_i. Sample one per disjoint stratum H with p_i>0 for all h_i nonzero. For targeted mixture p_i=alpha/|H|+(1-alpha)1[i in D]/|D| when D nonempty, otherwise uniform; alpha=.25. With independent draws across strata, E[HT|batch,past]=Delta by linearity, Var(HT|batch,past)=(100/n)^2 sum_H (sum_{i in H} h_i^2/p_i -(sum_{i in H}h_i)^2). First two zero-based positions cannot be selected at HORIZON, hence h_i=0 and exclusion unbiased for this contrast. For uniform vs targeted same strata, D contains all nonzero h; p_i targeted>=uniform on D, so conditional variance cannot increase (strict if nonzero contribution in stratum with D proper subset). This does NOT compare different strata/counts, guarantee individual estimates, or establish actual audited-policy advantage. Candidate sets/propensities must be frozen before audit outcomes; adaptive audit count may use past feedback. Window SE in THEMIS not a sequential confidence bound; repeated decisions, drift and adaptive observations invalidate naive coverage claims.
## T05: observational nonidentifiability
Construct worlds A/B with identical law of all available observations Z and independent algorithm randomization, but opposite optimal choices and equal gap delta>0. No feedback or other distinguishing signal is available. Any measurable decision based on Z/history has identical distribution in the two worlds. If it chooses context with probability p, expected regret is delta(1-p) in A and delta*p in B; worst >=delta/2, average exactly delta/2 under equal prior. This applies to a two-policy toy construction, not a universal quantitative bound on METIS. With distinguishable labels/telemetry/expensive outputs, premise may fail. Cheap-feature distribution alone cannot identify an arbitrary change in P(G|Z).
## T06: delay and evidence limits
For deterministic change at t0 and feedback delay D, first post-change label arrives no earlier than t0+D. With one estimate per batch and a W-batch full window, earliest all-post-change window t0+D+W-1 (assuming complete feedback). ARGOS D4,W8 gives t0+11; THEMIS D4,W16 gives t0+19. This is not earliest possible switching, not a guaranteed detection delay. Identical pre-feedback observations prohibit evidence-based distinction before delivery under T05 premises. Threshold, signal sparsity, sampling, window and policy persistence may make actual delay larger or infinite. No formal false-alarm or regret guarantee for current Judge.
